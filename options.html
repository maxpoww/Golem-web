The philosophy

OPTIONS

Why does a twelve-year-old master a video game in an afternoon, while adults fear their own computers for decades? Because game designers solved the problem desktop designers never did.

The lesson desktops never learned

Games teach complete novices to master complex systems — no manual, no training course, no fear of breaking anything. They do it with dynamic, diegetic, context-aware design: the right tool surfaces at the right moment, inside the world itself, and everything irrelevant simply is not there.

Desktops did the opposite. They put every capability on screen at once, buried the rest in menus and settings, wrote manuals nobody reads, and called the resulting fear "user error."

OPTIONS is the game designers' philosophy applied to an operating system. Help scales to your skill: scaffolding for the novice, silence for the expert, safety for both. If a feature needs a manual, we consider it unfinished.

How it works

Golem is one organism with four organs:

🧠

The Brain options-engine

A context engine. Collectors sense the world — your windows, media, git repos, phone, battery. A Mind decides what is logical to offer right now, and — just as important — what to take away.

🌊

The Body waverunner

The one living surface: dock, openbox, pills, boxes, notifications. GPU-rendered, animated with intent, idle at rest — motion means something or doesn't happen.

⛰️

The Ground NixOS

The whole machine is intent written to a file. Atomic updates, reproducible installs, a timeline of generations you can always step back through. The system cannot rot.

📱

The Reach Golem for Android

The phone becomes a collector and a surface of the same Brain. Not an accessory app — an organ of the same organism.

When is a module done?

Every OPTIONS module — clipboard, battery, dictionary, network, phone — must pass the same six gates before we call it finished:

Sensed — the Brain can perceive it through a collector.
Decided — the Mind proposes it only when its use is logical, and it goes away when not.
Shown — it has a surface that follows the design language: motion, materials, light.
Wired to the Brain — driven by the engine, no duplicate hand-made plumbing.
Seen — verified live on screen, idle at rest, tests clean.
Frictionless — it appears at the right moment, gets out of the way, and never interrupts flow. If it needs a manual, it is not done.

What this replaces

Clippy interrupted you. Copilot upsells you. macOS hides things and hopes. Linux hands you the parts and wishes you luck. Focus modes make you schedule your own attention.

OPTIONS is none of these, because it starts from sensing, not from marketing: the Brain knows you're in a call, in a game, on battery, holding a link — and behaves accordingly. Help that is offered, never imposed. Silence that is earned, never configured.

The right tool, the moment you need it. Gone when you don't.

One surface. One Brain. Zero friction. For everyone.
